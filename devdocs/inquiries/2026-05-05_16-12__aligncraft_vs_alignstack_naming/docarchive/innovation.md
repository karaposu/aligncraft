## Phase 2 — Generate (all 7 mechanisms × 3 variations: generic / focused / contrarian)

### Mechanism 1: Lens Shifting (Framer)

**Generic — change the evaluation frame from "what the book preaches" to "what the book sells":**
A name's job isn't to summarize content; it's to convert browsers into buyers. Under a marketing-conversion frame, "AlignCraft" is more discoverable to the target market (devs searching for "AI alignment" + "stack" / "framework"). "AlignCraft" is less SEO-aligned. Verdict under this lens: AlignCraft wins.

**Focused — change the frame from "book title" to "movement name":**
A book title is read once; a movement name is repeated for years. "Software Craftsmanship" succeeded as a movement; "Agile Stack" never existed. Movements need verb-able names. AlignCraft can become "I practice AlignCraft." AlignCraft stays a thing-noun. Verdict under this lens: AlignCraft wins decisively.

**Contrarian — flip the frame from "name should match content" to "name should compensate for content":**
The book's body is heavy on patterns and tools — somewhat dry, technical, granular. A *softer* name like AlignCraft balances the technical body with a humane register. A name like AlignCraft *amplifies* the technical-ness, possibly to over-saturation. Counterintuitively, AlignCraft compensates. Verdict: AlignCraft.

---

### Mechanism 2: Combination (Generator)

**Generic — combine "Align" with concepts from adjacent domains:**
- Align + Engineering term: AlignCraft, AlignFrame, AlignKit
- Align + Practice term: AlignCraft, AlignWorks, AlignDojo
- Align + Process term: AlignFlow, AlignLoop, AlignOps
- Align + Place term: AlignLab, AlignForge, AlignField
- Align + Quality term: AlignCore, AlignWay, AlignBase
None of these produce a candidate that beats AlignCraft on substance-fit while matching its memorability.

**Focused — combine the book's two metaphors (stack + craft) into one name:**
Hybrid candidates: "StackCraft" (loses Align), "AlignCraftcraft" (clunky), "The AlignCraft Stack" (descriptive subtitle, not name). The cleanest hybrid is to *use both* — title: AlignCraft, subtitle: "The Six-Layer Alignment Stack for AI-Driven Action." This is a *combination innovation*: the title carries the practice metaphor, the subtitle preserves the structural metaphor. Both win.

**Contrarian — combine "Align" with a non-engineering anchor:**
What if the name borrows from somewhere unexpected? "AlignSmith" (practice + tradition), "AlignWright" (old craft term, archaic), "AlignField" (relational/spatial), "AlignForge" (heavy artisanship). AlignSmith is interesting — "blacksmith" sense, builds-by-hand, practical, gendered-neutral in modern usage. But it loses the Software Craftsmanship lineage that "AlignCraft" inherits for free. Net: doesn't beat AlignCraft.

### Mechanism 3: Inversion (Framer)

**Generic — invert "AlignCraft is better":**
"AlignCraft is *worse* because…" — what would have to be true? The body of the book would have to be primarily structural/architectural, not practiced. Inverting back: is the body actually that? No — sensemaking confirmed it's practice-heavy. The inversion fails to find supporting evidence; the original verdict survives.

**Focused — invert "the name should match the content":**
What if the name should *deliberately mismatch* the content to force the reader to discover it? Strong technical books sometimes do this (e.g., "Code" by Petzold isn't called "Computer Hardware Fundamentals"). Could AlignCraft work *as a deliberately understated technical name* for a richer practice book? Possible, but the user already named it "AlignCraft" without irony — there's no positioning leverage in retro-claiming a mismatch. Inversion does not save AlignCraft.

**Contrarian — invert at system level: "The book shouldn't have a coined-word name at all":**
Component-level inversion ("don't use AlignCraft") → System-level inversion ("don't use any Align* coinage — use a phrase title"). What if the right title is "Aligned" (one word) or "The Alignment Discipline" or "Six Layers"? Phrase titles like "The Pragmatic Programmer" outperform coined words for serious methodology books in many markets. Counter-test: phrase titles are harder to community-ize ("the Pragmatic Programmer community" is awkward; "the AlignCraft community" is clean). The user's question scope assumes a coined word; this inversion goes outside scope and is filed as future thought, not verdict-changer.

### Mechanism 4: Constraint Manipulation (Framer)

**Generic — what if we *remove* the constraint that "Align" must be the prefix?**
The thesis word is alignment, but it doesn't have to live in the title. Counter-examples: "Refactoring" by Fowler doesn't have "code" in the title; "Clean Code" does. The thesis word IN the title is a strong heuristic but not law. Removing the constraint enables: "Codified Practice," "The Disciplined AI Pair," "The Six Layers." None of these are clearly better; the constraint earns its keep.

**Focused — what if we *add* the constraint "the title must work as a verb in dev culture":**
"You should AlignCraft this codebase" — works (parallel to "you should refactor this"). "You should AlignCraft this codebase" — clunky (Stack-as-verb is awkward). Adding the verb-ability constraint sharply favors AlignCraft. This is a *new structural argument* that strengthens the verdict.

**Contrarian — what if we *add* the constraint "must signal both structure AND practice":**
This is the constraint the book actually has internally. No single suffix satisfies it. Resolution: title carries one, subtitle carries the other. "AlignCraft: The Six-Layer Alignment Stack" or "AlignCraft: The Craft of AI-Aligned Development" — either ordering works structurally, but the *title* gets the noun-phrase prominence. The book IS more practice than structure; therefore the title should be AlignCraft and the subtitle should mention layers. Same conclusion as Mechanism 2.

### Mechanism 5: Absence Recognition (Generator)

**Generic — what's missing from the candidate list?**
Sensemaking enumerated 10 candidates but didn't probe the "two-word coined" space: "AlignArt" (too pretentious), "AlignWay" (zen-vague), "AlignPath" (similar). Missing dimension: emotional names. "Alignment Discipline," "The Aligned Programmer," "Alignment Mastery." None beats AlignCraft for the same reason: they sacrifice brand-coinability.

**Focused — what's the absent argument *for* AlignCraft that hasn't been made?**
Sensemaking covered: "Stack literally maps to six layers" (acknowledged). What it didn't probe: "Stack" implies *swappability* — different layers for different contexts. The book has the property that practitioners pick which layers to invest in based on their setup. "AlignCraft" subtly signals "modular menu," which fits some readings of the book. This is a real structural argument *for* AlignCraft that wasn't fully made before. **New survivor: keep this for critique.**

**Contrarian — what's absent from the question itself?**
The user asked about AlignCraft vs AlignCraft. They didn't ask: "Should the book be renamed at all?" The default of *keeping* the existing name carries epistemic conservatism: changes have unknown costs, current state has revealed strengths. Absence-recognized: a "do nothing" option that wasn't on the table. **New survivor: keep this as the conservative anchor.**

### Mechanism 6: Domain Transfer (Generator)

**Generic — how do other methodology books name themselves?**
- Engineering register: "Refactoring," "Working Effectively with Legacy Code," "Clean Code"
- Craft register: "The Pragmatic Programmer," "The Software Craftsman," "Coders at Work"
- System register: "Domain-Driven Design," "Site Reliability Engineering"
- Acronym/coinage register: "TDD," "BDD," "DDD"

Methodologies that achieved community traction tend to use either descriptive phrases ("Refactoring") OR coined acronyms ("TDD"). Coined-word brands (with capital-cased compounds) are rarer in methodology books — more common in product/SaaS. AlignCraft and AlignCraft are both *brand-style* names, which is *unusual* for methodology books. This raises a domain-transfer question: should the book follow methodology-book conventions (descriptive title + subtitle) instead of brand-style coinage?

**Focused — what does the Software Craftsmanship community itself name things?**
"The Manifesto for Software Craftsmanship" (descriptive). "Craftsmanship Conferences" (descriptive). "Apprentice Patterns" (book by Hoover/Oshineye — descriptive). The community's idiom is *descriptive*, not coined-brand. So importing from this domain suggests a non-brand title might fit the audience. But — counter — within the user's stated constraint (a single coined name), AlignCraft inherits the lineage even if it's not the community's idiom.

**Contrarian — transfer from gaming/product domains:**
Minecraft, StarCraft, World of Warcraft — these are massively successful brand-style coinages with "Craft." The pattern works in adjacent domains. The dev community is gaming-adjacent. Transfer suggests AlignCraft will perform well as a brand even if Software Craftsmanship books typically use descriptive titles. **New argument: AlignCraft has cross-domain brand-strength.**

### Mechanism 7: Extrapolation (Generator)

**Generic — extrapolate the book's trajectory:**
Five years out, the book will be published, have a community, possibly spawn workshops/conferences/derivative content. Which name scales better as a multi-product brand?
- "AlignCraft 2.0," "AlignCraft Conference," "AlignCraft for Enterprise" — works, but reads as a tech-product line
- "AlignCraft Workshop," "AlignCraft Practitioners' Conference," "AlignCraft for Teams" — reads as a discipline brand, similar to "Agile Coach," "TDD Practitioner"
The discipline-brand feels more natural for a methodology that wants to grow.

**Focused — extrapolate language drift:**
"Stack" in dev culture is increasingly tied to specific runtime tech-stacks (Vercel, Supabase, etc.). In five years, "AlignCraft" might collide with the inevitable AI tooling vendor that names itself "AlignCraft" or "Alignment Stack" as a product. "AlignCraft" is in less-crowded vendor space. Extrapolation favors AlignCraft for trademark longevity (this is technically out of scope per sensemaking, but worth flagging).

**Contrarian — extrapolate to the "model converges" scenario:**
If model intelligence plateaus (a thesis the user has elsewhere argued), methodology becomes the differentiator. In that future, methodology books matter more, and craft-language regains primacy (the Software Craftsmanship movement re-energizes). AlignCraft is positioned for that future. AlignCraft is positioned for the current moment where infrastructure-language dominates. The user's own broader thesis suggests favoring AlignCraft.
